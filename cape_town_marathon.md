### Training Zones
Zones are calibrated for a 3:45 marathon goal (5:20/km) with a 3:40 stretch (5:13/km). Heart rate bands have been shifted upward to reflect your naturally elevated HR profile - your cardiovascular system consistently reads higher than average at all intensities, which is completely normal and not a fitness limitation. Pace remains the primary guide for quality sessions; HR is the primary guide for easy and recovery runs. Zone 4 tempo pace has been modestly adjusted to better reflect your lactate threshold, and Zone 5 intervals updated accordingly.

### Taper Phase (3 Weeks)
Volume drops progressively: ~65% in Week 9, ~40% in Week 10, ~25% in Race Week. Maintain some intensity to stay sharp. Trust the hay is in the barn. You may feel sluggish or heavy during the taper - this is normal. Don't try to test your fitness now.

**Fuelling strategy:** Take your first gel at 40-45 minutes, then every 30-35 minutes. Practice this in your long runs (especially the 32km and 36km runs). Use the same gels you'll use on race day. Sip water at every water point from 15km onwards.

### Key Reminders
* **Listen to your body.** If legs feel heavy or you're fighting a cold, swap a quality session for easy running. Consistency over intensity.
* **Must Love Hills is non-negotiable** - it builds the leg strength this plan is designed to develop. The hills on Tuesdays will directly improve your leg durability for the marathon.
* **Long runs are sacred.** Hit every long run. If you miss a midweek session, that's OK. Never skip the Saturday long run unless injured.
* **Practice your fuelling** in the 32km and 36km long runs. Same gels, same timing, same breakfast.
* **The taper is not optional.** You wanted 3 weeks - trust it. You'll feel sluggish and anxious. That means it's working.
* **Sleep is training.** Aim for 7-8 hours, especially in the peak and taper phases.
* **For quality sessions (Z4-Z5), use pace as your guide** - don't chase a heart rate number.
* **For easy runs, use HR as your guide** - if it's creeping above Z2, slow down regardless of pace.
* **Cadence:** aim for 170-175spm on easy runs. On tough or hilly efforts, focus on keeping turnover quick even when pace drops - this is a skill that improves with practice.
* **Race day: start slow.** The first 10km should feel almost too easy. The marathon doesn't start until 30km.
* **This plan is a guideline.** Adapt it to how you feel. The best training plan is the one you can execute consistently.
